**Short answer:**  
*Signals and Systems* is **not a universal core requirement** in undergraduate Computer‑Science (CS) programmes, but **many CS departments do offer it (or a closely‑related variant) as an elective or as part of a specialised track** (e.g., embedded systems, robotics, data‑science, computer‑architecture, or AI‑focused curricula).  

---

### Why the picture looks the way it does

| Aspect | What the evidence shows | Typical situation for CS students |
|--------|------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Official CS curriculum guidelines (ACM/IEEE)** | The ACM Computer Science Curricula 2013 and the newer *Computer Science 2020* framework list “Signal processing” as **optional** material under the “Mathematical Foundations” and “Systems” domains. | A CS program can choose to include it, but it is not mandated. |
| **Course‑catalog surveys (top‑ranked, mid‑tier, international)** | – At many U.S. schools (e.g., MIT, Stanford, CMU) the course *Signals and Systems* lives in the Electrical‑Engineering (EE) or Computer‑Engineering (CE) department.  <br>– Some schools (e.g., University of Washington, University of Illinois‑Urbana, Georgia Tech) list the same‑named course **as an elective for CS majors** or cross‑listed between EE and CS. <br>– In European programmes (e.g., ETH Zürich, Technical University of Denmark) the course is usually EE‑centric, but CS students can enroll if they choose a hardware‑oriented track. | CS students can usually **take the course** (often after getting departmental permission) but they do **not have to** for graduation. |
| **Typical CS degree maps** | Core CS blocks contain: programming, data structures, algorithms, theory of computation, computer architecture, operating systems, databases, and software engineering.  “Signals and Systems” appears **only in optional clusters** such as “Embedded & Real‑time Systems”, “Multimedia & Vision”, or “Machine‑Learning Foundations”. | If a CS major elects a hardware‑oriented or signal‑processing track, the course becomes a **recommended elective**; otherwise it is omitted. |
| **Textbooks & prerequisites** | Standard textbooks (e.g., Oppenheim & Willsky *Signals and Systems*, S. Haykin *Communication Systems*) are listed for EE/CE courses.  Prerequisites usually include calculus, linear algebra, and a basic programming or discrete‑math class—subjects that CS students already have. | CS students who have taken the required math courses can **handle the material** without extra effort. |
| **Literature on CS education trends** | Recent surveys (e.g., *IEEE Transactions on Education* 2021, *ACM Computing Surveys* 2022) note a **growing interest** in exposing CS students to signal‑processing concepts, especially for AI, robotics, and embedded‑systems curricula.  However, the majority of CS programmes still keep the course **outside the core**. | The trend is toward **more elective offerings**, not core mandatory status. |

---

### Practical take‑aways for a CS student

| Situation | What to do |
|-----------|------------|
| **Your programme has a “Signal Processing” or “Signals and Systems” elective** | Enroll if you plan to work in areas like embedded systems, audio/video processing, robotics, or data‑science that rely on time‑domain/ frequency‑domain analysis. |
| **The course is listed only in the EE/CE catalogue** | Most universities allow cross‑registration; check with the CS advisor for permission. |
| **Your curriculum has no mention of it** | You can still self‑study using the standard textbooks or online resources (e.g., MIT OpenCourseWare, NPTEL). The material aligns well with courses you already take (probability, linear algebra, algorithms). |
| **You want it as a requirement for a specific CS specialization** | Look for CS programmes that explicitly include “Signals and Systems” in a **hardware, robotics, or AI track** (e.g., CS + EE joint majors, “Computer Systems” concentrations). |

---

### Bottom line

- **Core requirement?** **No** – most CS undergraduate degree requirements do not list *Signals and Systems* as a required course.
- **Elective / optional?** **Yes** – it is widely offered as an elective, often cross‑listed with EE/CE, and is encouraged for CS students whose interests lie in hardware, multimedia, or AI‑related fields.
- **Availability:** Almost all universities that have an EE or CE department **offer the course**, and CS students can usually take it after satisfying the basic math prerequisites.

So, **if you are a CS student and want to study Signals and Systems, you most likely can, but you will usually need to select it as an elective or arrange a cross‑departmental enrollment rather than it being a mandatory part of the CS core curriculum.**

## 6. Quick “What to Do If …”

| Situation | Immediate Action |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Attendance shortfall** | Apply for a **“Attendance Relaxation”** to the respective department head (deadline: 2 Nov). |
| **Fee not reflected** | Check payment receipt; raise a ticket on the portal and inform the Finance Office. |
| **Hall‑ticket not generated** | Verify that registration is complete; contact Examination Cell with your roll‑no. |
| **Exam venue change** | The updated venue will be reflected on the hall‑ticket; keep an eye on email alerts 48 hrs before the exam. |
| **Medical emergency during exam** | Inform the invigilator immediately; a medical certificate will be required for a re‑evaluation request. |

Download the relevant PDFs **at least two weeks before the exam** to focus your revision.
